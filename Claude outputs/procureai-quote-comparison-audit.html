02Findings, in the buyer's order of questions

Thirteen questions, checked against the actual code — not the intent.

01
Are all supplier quotes displayed accurately?
Yes. rfq.quotes keeps every submission; addQuote replaces a supplier's own prior quote in place rather than dropping anyone, and the comparison view renders every entry with no filtering.
Works
02
Is quoted quantity preserved?
Yes. Quote.quotedQuantity is stored exactly as the supplier entered it and shown as its own field — never silently rounded or overwritten.
Works
03
Are unit price, shipping, GST, and other costs calculated correctly?
The arithmetic itself is correct — tax = subtotal × taxPercent / 100, rounded into a total. But it only runs once, inside awardRFQ, for the single quote the buyer already picked. Every other quote on the table is compared without ever having its real cost computed.
PartialP0
04
Is total cost calculated consistently?
No. scoreQuotes() ranks purely on unitPrice — shipping and tax never enter the score or the “Recommended” badge. A supplier with a low unit price and expensive shipping can rank above a genuinely cheaper landed-cost option, and nothing in the UI would show that.
MissingP0
05
Can different quote quantities be compared fairly?
Unit price alone is a fair per-unit comparison. But shippingCost is a flat fee, not per-unit — at very different order quantities, that flat fee changes the real per-unit landed cost by very different amounts, and nothing normalizes for it.
WeakP0
06
Are lead time and deadline compatibility considered?
leadTimeScore only ranks quotes against each other (fastest = 100) — it never checks a single one against the buyer's actual requested deadline, even though that data (structuredRequirement.deliveryTimeframe) already exists on the RFQ since the P0 work. The “fastest” quote can still miss the buyer's real deadline with no warning.
WeakP1
07
Is MOQ compatibility considered?
No. quote.moq is displayed as a number and nothing more. A supplier whose minimum order quantity is higher than what's actually being ordered can still be scored and recommended as the top choice — a quote that is structurally impossible to fulfill looks identical to one that isn't.
MissingP0
08
Are payment terms and quote validity displayed?
Payment terms: yes, shown and scored. Quote validity: the date is shown as plain text, but never checked against today — an expired quote carries no visual flag and can be awarded exactly like a live one.
PartialP1
09
Are missing fields clearly marked as unavailable?
Two concrete instances say otherwise. An unfilled MOQ renders as —, indistinguishable from a supplier who explicitly stated “no minimum.” Worse, an unfilled shipping cost renders as the word Included — turning silence into a claim the supplier never made. This is the one finding that directly conflicts with “unavailable information must not be treated as confirmed compliance,” independent of anything else in this milestone.
WeakP0
10
Is the AI recommendation explainable?
recommendationReason() does produce an honest, plain-language reason (“lowest unit price, fastest lead time”) for exactly what it reasoned over — the problem is what it reasons over is incomplete (no total cost, no MOQ or deadline check, no supplier evidence or performance), and it never says why the alternatives were passed over.
PartialP1
11
Can the buyer override the recommendation?
Yes, already — every quote carries its own “Award & generate PO” button, not just the top-ranked one, so the buyer was never railroaded. What's missing is a record of it: nothing captures whether the eventual award matched or overrode the AI's pick.
WorksP1 (traceability)
12
Is the award decision clearly recorded?
The PO captures the winning quote's terms, but not the decision itself — which other quotes existed, their scores, or why this one won. If the scoring logic changes later, there's no way to reconstruct why a past award was made. Separately: awardRFQ has no server-side guard against being called twice on the same RFQ — the UI hides the button after award, but the API would silently overwrite the PO if hit again directly.
PartialP1
13
Does the generated PO reflect the awarded quote accurately?
Unit price, quantity, payment terms, and delivery date are all copied faithfully from the winning quote. The gap is transparency: totalValue is shown as one rolled-up number with no visible subtotal, tax, or shipping line — the buyer can't verify the math without redoing it by hand.
WorksP1 (transparency)

03Price intelligence — what the data actually supports

Checked against real fields in data/rfqs-store.json, not aspiration. Nothing below assumes data that doesn't exist.

Metric Status What the audit found
Current quoted price Available Real, per quote, already stored and displayed.
Total landed cost Buildable now Every input (unit price, quantity, tax %, shipping) already exists — it's just never computed until after the decision (see finding 04). Straightforward to add per quote, pre-award.
Lowest quoted price Buildable now Trivial min() across an RFQ's own quotes. Not currently surfaced as a callout — the buyer has to eyeball the list.
Average quoted price Buildable now Same — trivial to compute across an RFQ's own quotes, not currently shown.
Historical ProcureAI quoted price (product/category benchmark) Insufficient data The real store currently holds a handful of RFQs total, and there's no product/category-matching logic to group comparable historical quotes even once volume exists. Should honestly read “Insufficient data” until both are true — not approximated in the meantime.
Potential savings Buildable now, narrow Honest version only: “X% below the highest quote received on this RFQ.” That's real, internal, available data. A “vs. market” savings figure is not possible without an external price source and must not be attempted.
Price benchmark (external / market) Out of scope No external market-price source exists in this codebase. Doing this honestly means not doing it yet — “Insufficient data” is the correct answer, not a gap to quietly fill with an estimate.
Supplier-specific price history Partially available lib/supplier-performance.ts already computes one aggregate — averageQuotedPrice — per supplier, across all their historical quotes. There's no visible list of individual past quotes yet, and the aggregate mixes whatever products that supplier has quoted on, so it should be labeled “average across N historical quotes, may span different products,” not implied as a clean apples-to-apples figure.

04Prioritized work

Grouped by what blocks a trustworthy decision versus what strengthens one. Nothing here has been implemented.

P0 — blocks a trustworthy decision

Without these, "Recommended" can point at the wrong supplier and nothing would catch it.
  • Compute total landed cost (unitPrice × quantity + tax + shipping) per quote at comparison time, not only at award.
  • Fold total cost — not unit price alone — into scoreQuotes() and the "Recommended" badge.
  • Check each quote's MOQ against the requested/quoted quantity; flag or exclude quotes that structurally can't be fulfilled.
  • Stop rendering an unfilled shipping cost as “Included” — show it as “Not specified,” same as any other missing field.
  • Add a server-side guard in awardRFQ against awarding an RFQ that's already been awarded.

P1 — materially strengthens trust

Not launch-blocking, but each one closes a real gap between "looks right" and "is right."
  • Compare each quote's lead time against the buyer's actual requested deadline (structuredRequirement.deliveryTimeframe), not just against other quotes.
  • Flag a quote whose validUntil date has already passed.
  • Surface lowest and average quoted price for the current RFQ as explicit callouts.
  • Show "savings vs. other quotes on this RFQ" — never vs. invented market data.
  • Show the PO's cost breakdown (subtotal / tax / shipping / total), not only the final rolled-up number.
  • Record the comparison context at award time — the scores and alternatives that existed at that moment — so a past decision stays explainable even if scoring logic later changes.
  • Weave supplier evidence and derived performance (from the P0 work) into the recommendation where it exists, clearly labeled as ProcureAI-derived — never as credit for merely being in the database.
  • Add a real per-supplier quote history list, not just the single aggregate average.

P2 — genuinely blocked on data or scope

Correctly deferred, not neglected — building these now would mean guessing.
  • Product/category-level historical price benchmarking across all RFQs — needs real quote volume and product-matching logic that doesn't exist yet.
  • External market price benchmarking — no market-data source exists; do not approximate one.
  • A structured way for a supplier to affirmatively confirm a specific requirement (a certification, a material) — today that's simply unknown, which is the honest state, but a real acknowledgment mechanism would be a genuine future improvement.

AI recommends. Evidence proves. Human decides.

A low price should never automatically read as the best supplier. The recommendation above should weigh total cost, quantity and MOQ compatibility, delivery against the real deadline, quality requirements, supplier evidence and performance, payment terms, and quote validity together — and whatever isn't known must stay visibly unknown, never quietly treated as satisfied.
